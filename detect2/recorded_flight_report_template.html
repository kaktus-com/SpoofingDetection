01 / The first constraint

Measurement time and arrival time disagree.

The receiver’s GPS epoch clock advances 71.72 seconds while the recorded arrival clock advances 102.09 seconds. A nearby attitude arrival timestamp cannot repair that delay. The plots compare recorded arrival timing with an epoch-based candidate time axis.

The current mapping is PX4 boot = __ANCHOR__ + GPS elapsed + __OFFSET__ seconds. Its anchor comes from the earliest receive-time proxy. Offset status: __CLOCK_STATUS__. Discovery-only motion consistency can estimate an offset, but does not supply an external timing calibration. The scale is assumed to be one second per second. No offset is marked as verified.

The exploratory check learns one unconstrained phase response per signal from discovery windows and predicts separate holdout windows. Preferred discovery offset: __DISCOVERY__ s; preferred holdout offset: __HOLDOUT__ s. Agreement alone would not establish synchronization; unconstrained response vectors, vibration and timing error can produce misleading fits. This check does not compute the paper’s likelihood ratio.

02 / Independent motion measurements

Supplied attitude. Our displacement integration.

Measured FRD specific force is rotated by the PX4 quaternion, converted from NED to ENU and combined with gravity. Initial position and velocity are zero in each short window. No PX4 fused displacement or gyro attitude estimator is used. __LEVER__

Interpolation excludes attitude gaps above 75 ms and reset transitions. The existing integrator also rejects IMU gaps above 1.5 nominal sample intervals; missing samples are not silently filled. Detected attitude reset at PX4 boot: __RESETS__ s.

__IMU_PLOT__

Supplied PX4 attitude is an estimate and may depend on the flight controller’s aiding configuration. No extra EKF accelerometer-bias estimate is subtracted. Grounded adjacent-sample noise proxy by body axis: __NOISE_PROXY__ m/s². This short ground segment does not calibrate in-flight vibration, coloured noise or attitude uncertainty.
